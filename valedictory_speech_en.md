# NTU CCDS Graduation Speech 2025

Theme: "AI is just code"
Date: 2025-07-22
Deliverer: Ng Tze Kean

## Greetings

Presiding Officer, Mr Desmond Lee, Minister for Education,
Members of the Academic Procession,
Distinguished Guests,
Fellow Graduates,
Ladies and Gentlemen,
My name is Ng Tze Kean, and I am a graduate of the Computer Science Programme.

## Introduction

It felt like yesterday we were frantically debugging our code at 3 AM, fueled by coffee and the sheer terror of a looming deadline. And now, here we are. It’s truly an honour, a privilege, to be up here representing all of us, the NTU CCDS Class of 2025. Our journey together has been a really wild and fun one, hasn’t it?

From the day we stepped onto this campus, we were told that the world outside our lecture halls is dynamic. The tools we’re expected to use, ever morphing. But today, as we stand on the verge of a new chapter, I want to talk about something that's on everyone's mind, a shadow looming over our bright futures: Artificial Intelligence.

And I want to tell you, with all the conviction in my heart, that the Class of 2025 will not be threatened by AI. Why? Because AI is just code.

Now, some of you might be thinking I'm just oversimplifying things. But hear me out.

## What we overcame

Remember when we first started, in the thick of COVID-19? Our "freshie experience" was virtual orientation, staring at screens for lessons, trying to bond over Microsoft Teams. Then, slowly but surely, we transitioned back. From work-from-home to hybrid arrangements, then back to the office, back to physical lessons. The simple joy of bumping into a friend in North Spine, grabbing a quick lunch at Canteen, or just having a proper face-to-face discussion in a project group – these things were suddenly precious again.

Physical interaction, true human connection, was brought back to us.

Now, in this new era of AI, some might fear that this human element, this connection, will once again be taken away. That our jobs will be automated, our creativity replaced. But to that, I say: Of course not!

## What we achieved

Our education here in NTU, especially in CCDS, has been more than just learning syntax and algorithms. It’s been our springboard, providing us with that solid foundation to do big things. We've been taught to think critically, to problem-solve, to adapt.

We’ve triumphed over all the challenges that came our way, from those complex machine learning assignments that pushed our limits, to those collaborative group projects that truly tested our teamwork. We persevered through all of it, and this itself is a testament to our grit, resolve, and determination as students of NTU CCDS.

Look around you! We have classmates who ventured into cutting-edge research, applying generative AI in the healthcare sector to help doctors diagnose illnesses faster, or even in education to personalize learning for students. We have friends from our High Performance Computing team who went overseas for competitions, pushing the boundaries of what’s possible, exploring uncharted frontiers to advance the scientific community.

These achievements aren't just lines of code; they are solutions and innovations, driven by our passion and our intelligence. Each one of us here today has demonstrated our technical prowess, not just as coders, but as leaders who will change the world.

## What's next

Now here is some food for thought. We often measure contributions to our assignments by the number of lines of code that were typed, or the number of commits on GitHub. And yes, those are important metrics in our field. But if we are to go forth, to truly make a big change, to build a better future, I urge you: measure others, and indeed measure yourselves, by the size of your heart. That, my friends, is what truly makes us human after all.

Because at the end of the day, AI, no matter how sophisticated, is still a reflection of human intelligence, human creativity, and human intent. It’s built and guided by us. Like I said, AI is just code. And we, the humans, are the ones who write it, who understand its limitations, and who ultimately decide how it’s used.

## Expressing gratitude

Before I conclude, I would like to express our deepest thanks to those who have supported us. To our incredible professors, who not only taught us how to code, but more importantly, taught us how to think, how to question, and how to never-stop-learning. To our friends, who shared this journey with us, celebrated our wins, and helped us through every late-night debugging session. And to our parents and loved ones, our guiding stars, who provided unwavering love and support these four years.

The Class of 2025 would now like to dedicate a Class Gift to the University. This class gift will support financially challenged students, drive school advancement, enhance student life, and improve the quality of education. This year, close to 3000 students from the graduating cohort supported the philanthropic initiative. This class gift, which marks their graduation, is more than just a cheque. It is an expression of their appreciation towards NTU, and it also celebrates their spirit of giving.

As a former recipient of the NTU Bursary Endowment Fund, I am deeply grateful to everyone who took part in Graduation Giving. I am honoured to present our Graduation Class Gift, on behalf of the Class of 2025, to our President, Prof Ho.

## Ending

If there's one thing I want etched in your minds today, it's this: AI is just code. Just as a young Steve Jobs and Bill Gates ushered in the digital age from analog typewriter and manual cabinets, we – the Class of 2025 – stand at the precipice of an equally profound shift, ready to embrace and shape the AI era.

So as we step forward, remember: AI is just code. But we are the heart, the soul, and the boundless vision behind the innovation.

Congratulations, Class of 2025—our future is unwritten, and it’s ours to code.
Thank you.
